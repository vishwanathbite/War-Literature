01 · CONCEPT NOTE

Literature as witness.
War as question.

From epic conflict to mechanised and technologically mediated violence, the conference examines how literature records, interrogates and reimagines war and its aftermath.

War is older than civilization itself—and literature has been bearing witness to it since the beginning. From The Iliad and the Mahabharata to Beowulf, the epic tradition was forged in the crucible of conflict. As the machinery of violence grew sharper, more calculated and more impersonal, literature increasingly turned from battlefield valour towards psychological anguish, displacement, silence and loss.

The conference asks how war is represented when conflict is fought not only over territory but also over ideologies, identities and abstractions. It approaches violence, death, displacement, trauma and demolition as lived realities rather than distant abstractions.

Particular attention is given to classical Indian perspectives. In the Ramayana, Mahabharata, Shantiparva and Bhagavad Gita, conflict is entangled with Dharma, ethical duty, moral discernment, Dharmayuddha, reconciliation and Lokasangraha. The conference places these perspectives in dialogue with contemporary theories and literary traditions.

Government Vidarbha Institute of Science & Humanities, Amravati

Founded in 1923, GVISH is presented in the brochure as a major centre for teaching and research in the Vidarbha region. The institute has UGC-granted autonomy and an 'A' NAAC grade with a CGPA of 3.51. It teaches from the eleventh standard through doctorate level, with 21 undergraduate and 19 postgraduate subjects and recognised Ph.D. research centres in 20 subjects.

Department of English

English has been taught at GVISH since the institute first opened. The Department of English dates from 1957, when English was introduced as a special subject at graduate level; postgraduate study followed in 1962. The department offers B.A., B.Sc. compulsory English, M.A., M.Phil. and Ph.D. programmes.
